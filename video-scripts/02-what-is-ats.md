# Video 2: "What Is an ATS?"

**Target length:** ~75 seconds (~160 words)
**Tone:** myth-busting, calm, a little reassuring
**Note:** condensed from the existing Learn article `frontend/articles/what-is-ats.md` — keep the "filing cabinet, not a bouncer" metaphor, since that's the site's established explanation.

---

**[VISUAL: A resume walks up to a big scary robot bouncer at a velvet rope. Robot menacingly crosses arms.]**

You've probably heard a robot reads your resume before a human does — and pictured something like this. Scary, right?

**[VISUAL: Bouncer robot deflates into a friendly filing cabinet with a search bar on it.]**

Here's the truth: an ATS — Applicant Tracking System — is a filing cabinet with a search bar, not a bouncer. It stores applications and lets a recruiter search and sort them.

**[VISUAL: Papers going into cabinet get sorted into neat labeled folders — Name, Dates, Skills.]**

It reads your resume into fields — name, job titles, dates, skills — so a recruiter can find you.

**[VISUAL: A two-column resume gets fed in sideways and scrambles; a single-column version goes in clean.]**

Most ATS problems aren't about missing keywords — they're parsing problems. Fancy columns, text boxes, and graphics can confuse it. Plain and clearly labeled beats clever.

**[VISUAL: Robot filing cabinet hands a folder to a human, who reads it and smiles.]**

The ATS doesn't decide if you're hired — it just makes sure a human can find you. Write for the human. Format for the machine.

**[TEXT ON SCREEN: Clear beats clever. Every time.]**
