**[VISUAL: A resume with a skill word being typed in, then a red flag popping up next to it — "you don't actually have this yet."]**

When a job posting asks for a skill you don't have, the tempting move is to just... type the word in anyway. Don't. A resume that says you have a skill you don't is a problem waiting for your first day on the job.

**[VISUAL: Same resume, but now the skill word fades, replaced by a small graduation cap icon.]**

The real fix takes longer, but it's not out of reach. Sites like Coursera, edX, and LinkedIn Learning have real courses — often free, or free with a library card. Your local community college likely has affordable continuing-education classes, some just a few weeks long.

**[VISUAL: Calendar flips forward a few weeks; graduation cap appears next to the skill word on the resume, now genuinely earned.]**

Put the skill on your resume once it's actually true — and it'll hold up in the interview, not just get you past the ATS.
